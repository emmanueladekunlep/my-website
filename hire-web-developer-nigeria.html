The Problem

Why hiring a web developer in Nigeria goes wrong so often

It's not bad luck. It's predictable. When the market has no standards, business owners pay the price — sometimes twice. These are the failures that repeat.

⚠

Unverifiable skill

Anyone can call themselves a developer. Some have never shipped a real project. Without a live portfolio or a reference call, you can't tell skill from confidence.

⚠

No written agreements

"We agreed on WhatsApp" sounds fine until scope, price or timeline is disputed. Without a written scope, nothing is enforceable and everything is emotional.

⚠

Payment upfront, delivery never

Full payment requested before work begins is the most common trap. When the developer disappears, you have no leverage — no code, no refund, no recourse.

⚠

Code held hostage

Even when the site is delivered, some developers keep the code, hosting or domain in their name. You don't own your own business asset — you rent it.

⚠

Templates sold as custom work

A cheap template configured in a day is sold as bespoke development. It looks fine until you need changes, integrations or scalability — then reality bites.

⚠

No post-launch support

Site goes live, developer vanishes. Bugs appear, security patches are missed, small changes become impossible. The website becomes a liability instead of an asset.

The Solution

How to hire a web developer in Nigeria — a practical checklist

Before you send money to anyone, run through this list. A serious developer passes all of it without hesitation. If they resist any point, walk away.

  • ✓
    Ask for live links, not screenshots. Screenshots can be faked. Live websites can't. Open them on your own phone — test speed, mobile experience and whether they actually work.
  • ✓
    Get a written scope before paying anything. Features, deliverables, timeline, price. If they can't put it in writing, they can't deliver it in reality.
  • ✓
    Insist on milestone payments. A 30/40/30 or 40/30/30 structure is standard. Full payment upfront is a red flag. No payment at all upfront is also unrealistic — you want aligned incentives.
  • ✓
    Confirm who owns the code and assets. In writing: source code, hosting, domain, content, design files. Everything should be in your name or handed over on completion.
  • ✓
    Ask about post-launch support. Bug fixes, minor updates, security patches. "Nothing included" is acceptable if stated clearly. Silence isn't.
  • ✓
    Speak to at least one past client. Not a testimonial on their website — an actual conversation. Anyone worth hiring has clients happy to vouch for them.
  • ✓
    Check if they can explain things simply. Good developers explain technical decisions without jargon. If they can't make you understand, they can't make your system maintainable either.
  • ✕
    Walk away if any of these appear: full payment demanded upfront, no written scope, no live portfolio, no willingness to share code, no references, no clear identity or business name.

FAQ

Straight answers before you hire anyone

How much should I pay a web developer in Nigeria?

There is no single price. A simple brochure website, a business site with SEO and lead capture, and a full web application sit at very different price points. What matters more than the figure is what the figure includes: design, development, SEO, hosting, training and post-launch support. Be wary of quotes far below the market — they usually mean templates dressed up as custom work or missing essential elements. I quote per project after a short discovery call and break every proposal into clear milestones.

What are the red flags when hiring a web developer in Nigeria?

Common red flags include: no portfolio of live links to real projects, reluctance to show previous work, no written scope before payment, requesting full payment upfront, no GitHub or version control, no way to speak to past clients, and disappearing for days with no communication. A serious developer will happily show live work, explain their stack, provide references and structure payment around milestones.

Should I hire a freelance developer or an agency in Nigeria?

Both can work. Agencies offer teams but charge more and often assign junior staff to smaller projects. Freelancers offer direct access to the person doing the work at better value, but you must verify quality. A third option: hire a founder-developer like me — I run a registered company (LabelReach Advertising Ltd), work hands-on, and have both business and technical experience.

What should be included in a web development contract?

A fair contract should include: a clear written scope of work, defined deliverables, milestone-based payment schedule, timeline with key dates, what happens if scope changes, who owns the code on completion, what post-launch support is included, and how disputes are handled. If a developer resists written agreements, that itself is a red flag — no matter how friendly they are.
