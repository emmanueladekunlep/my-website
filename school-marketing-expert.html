The Problem

Why school marketing in Nigeria keeps failing

Marketing isn't the problem. Unstructured, unmeasured, agency-driven marketing is the problem. These are the patterns I see in almost every school that approaches me.

⚠

Marketing without strategy

Flyers here, Instagram there, a banner at a church event. Random activity that doesn't build toward anything. Parents see noise, not a school worth choosing.

⚠

Generic agencies doing school work

An agency used to selling products treats your school like a product launch. But parents don't buy education on impulse — they research, ask, visit and deliberate. School marketing is different.

⚠

No measurement at all

Most schools can't tell you how many enquiries they got last term, where they came from, or how many became pupils. What isn't measured can't be improved.

⚠

Marketing that stops at awareness

You can flood the market with your name and still lose every parent at the follow-up stage. Awareness without a conversion system just wastes money.

⚠

Social media treated as the whole strategy

Social media is one channel. It works best when it supports a stronger positioning, website, tour experience and referral engine — not when it's the entire plan.

⚠

No owner-level accountability

You pay an agency or a social media handler, but there's no one accountable for the outcome. Marketing becomes a line item rather than a growth system.

FAQ

What school owners ask about school marketing

What does a school marketing expert actually do?

A school marketing expert builds and runs the systems that bring pupils into your school. That includes: defining your positioning, creating campaigns, managing social media, improving Google visibility, running paid ads where sensible, building WhatsApp admissions funnels, designing open-day campaigns and tracking where every pupil actually came from. It's measured by enrolment — not by likes, followers or impressions.

How is school marketing different from normal business marketing?

School marketing operates on a longer trust cycle. Parents don't buy education the way they buy shoes — they research, ask other parents, visit, compare and decide carefully. That means school marketing must focus on credibility, proof, reputation and relationships — not impulse. A generic marketing agency without school experience usually gets this wrong.

How much should a Nigerian school spend on marketing?

Most private schools under-spend dramatically on marketing and over-spend on rent, unbranded flyers and one-off activities. A sensible benchmark is to treat marketing as a percentage of termly fee income rather than a fixed naira figure. I recommend a realistic budget after an enrolment audit — because spending more on the wrong things won't help.

Do you handle social media for schools?

Yes. Social media is one channel within a school marketing system — not the whole thing. I manage or advise on school social media content, posting calendars, community engagement and paid promotion. The goal is always measurable enquiries and enrolments, not follower counts.

Can you guarantee my school will get more pupils?

No honest marketer can guarantee a specific number of pupils — because many factors are outside anyone's control (location, competition, fees, facilities, reputation). What can be guaranteed is a proper system: clear positioning, a real marketing plan, measurable campaigns, consistent follow-up and tracking. When that system is in place, growth becomes far more likely and far more predictable.

How do you measure school marketing success?

By the only metrics that matter: number of enquiries, number of school tours, number of enrolments, average fee per pupil, cost per enrolment, and parent referral rate. Likes and impressions are diagnostic — not goals. Every campaign I run is measured against pupil outcomes.
